Leonida's Map Is Bigger Than You Think — Here's How It Compares

Vice City is only the headline. We measured islands, highways, and the Keys against San Andreas and Los Santos — and the playable landmass is a different conversation.

September 21, 2026

Rico Alvarez

Maps

2 min read0%

Share
MAPSLeonida's Map Is Bigger Than You Think — Here's How It Compares

Vice City looks infinite from a helicopter. From a motorcycle, it is a grid with excellent lighting. The real story of Grand Theft Auto VI's map is not the skyline — it is Leonida, the state wrapped around that skyline, and how much of it you can actually use.

This desk spent a week driving, boating, and stalling in traffic with a stopwatch. We are not claiming a leaked file dump. We are claiming something more useful: a playable-size comparison against San Andreas (2004), Los Santos (2013), and the densest slices of Red Dead's world.

What "bigger" actually means

Rockstar markets acreage. Players feel density, draw distance, and whether a highway is a corridor or a place.

Leonida is not a bigger Los Santos. It is a smaller Florida that happens to contain a maximalist Vice City.

That distinction matters. Los Santos compressed a fantasy Los Angeles into a ring of mountains. Leonida stretches: barrier islands, inland suburbs, agricultural grid, swamp, and a Keys chain that is not a loading-screen joke.

Vice City versus the rest of the state

Inside the city proper, block-to-block density is the highest Rockstar has shipped. Alleys connect. Parking structures are not cardboard. Storefronts have interiors you will actually enter because the economy wants you there.

Outside the city, the map changes grammar:

  • North-east industrial — Port Gellhorn's cranes, yards, and a customs energy that exists to stage heists.
  • Grassrivers — slow water, fast ambushes, wildlife that is not decoration.
  • The Keys — a string of causeways where weather and fuel start to matter.
  • Inland towns — churches, pawn shops, and county cops who do not run Vice City's script.

How it compares to San Andreas and Los Santos

San Andreas won on variety per minute. You could leave Los Santos and be in countryside, desert, then San Fierro before a podcast episode ended. Los Santos won on fidelity per block and lost the rest of the state.

Leonida tries to hold both. Early hours will trick you into thinking the city is the whole game. It is not. The state's rural third is where Jason's comfort and Lucia's impatience become a travel mechanic.

Pro Tip

If you are mapping for yourself, ignore tourist helicopters. Rent a dirty bike, stay off highways, and count unique interiors in a twenty-minute radius. Density is a local statistic.

Travel time is the hidden difficulty

A map this long changes mission design. Getaway routes can be states. That is why several story jobs now include ferry windows and dual-vehicle swaps — see our Keys Connector notes in Mission Intel.

The wanted system also reads distance differently. County lines are not cosmetic. Lose the Vice City cops and you may still inherit a slower, meaner rural unit that knows the dirt roads.

Spoiler

What we are not claiming

We are not publishing leaked dimension numbers as gospel. Trailer analysis and in-world travel times can lie. Storm cells hide pop-in. Water tiles inflate screenshots.

What we will say, editorially: if Los Santos was a city with a ring, Leonida is a region with a capital. Vice City is the neon headline. The map is the story.

Drive it yourself

Park at dusk on the causeway facing the city. Then turn around. The dark water, the radio hiss, the next island's motel sign — that is the size. Not the skyline. The turn.
