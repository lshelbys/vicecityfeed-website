The garage in Grand Theft Auto VI is not a pause menu with chrome. It is a room. It has a radio, a parts cage, a lift that takes real time, and a door that opens onto whichever district you actually live in.

That last part is the revolution. Customization is local.

## Stance, weather, and underglow that works in rain

Los Santos liveries were stickers. Vice City's paint stack includes pearl under rain, neon that blooms correctly in puddles, and stance that changes how a car *reads* to cops at a checkpoint.

> If your build only looks good in the garage lighting, it is not finished. Take it to the causeway in a storm.

## A parts economy that can bankrupt you

You cannot buy every option from a single catalog. Body kits lock behind shops, races, and a handful of story beats. That will annoy completionists. It will delight people who want their first Vice City coupe to feel earned.

:::protip
Save a "clean" civilian spec of every daily driver. High-vis underglow is a rolling wanted star in residential north Vice after 1 a.m. The game does not say this. The cops do.
:::

## Performance is no longer a single slider

Engine swaps, tire compounds, and brake bias matter on the Keys' long causeways. A slammed show car that slaps in the city will wash out on wet bridges. Build twice: city and state.

## Why modders should be watching the pipeline

The livery editor exports in a way that looks intentionally community-shaped. Layers, editable manufacturer plates, and underglow presets are the kind of hooks that, on PC, become a thousand garage packs.

We wrote a separate briefing on the post-launch mod scene. The short version: if Rockstar ships the toolchain even halfway open, Vice City becomes the most photographed fictional city of the decade.

:::spoiler
A hidden "showroom" flag on certain marina boats lets you preview liveries without owning the hull. Combined with the photo mode grid, it is essentially an in-engine livery tester — the thing mod teams usually have to fake.
:::

## The garage as hideout

You will spend story downtime here. Jason wipes down a bumper. Lucia changes a station. The lift ticks. That is world-building you cannot put in a trailer, and it is why this desk is treating Garage & Mods as a vertical, not a sidebar.
